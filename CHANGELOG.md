# CHANGELOG

## 0.4.1

- Character builder: a search box next to "Available" on the Race, Abilities and Skills steps filters the list to pick from as you type. Every word typed has to appear in the entry's name or the note beside it, so a group or a stat such as "MIG" finds entries too. Headings with nothing left under them are hidden. Each step keeps its own search, and it stays after adding something.
- Characters' tokens are now linked to the character by default, so every token is the same character as its sheet. Before, Foundry made each token its own copy of the character. One symptom was the health and stamina bars on such a token not updating for the first change after the page was reloaded, such as a breather or paying stamina for an ability. New characters get linked tokens, and when the GM loads the world, existing characters' prototype tokens are switched to linked. Tokens already placed on a scene aren't changed, since an unlinked token may have changes of its own. To link one, replace it with a new token from the character, or tick Link Actor Data in its token settings.
- Breather: conditions are now cleared before health and stamina are restored, so health and stamina are filled to their maximum once the conditions are gone.
- Character sheet: only the GM can change a character's Speed now, like PDR and MDR. Players see it locked. It still shows the base Speed plus racial skill bonuses such as Faster Movement.
- Abilities: when an effect lowers an ability's stat below the ability's minimum rank, the ability is now disabled instead of being used at a rank it doesn't have. For example, Counterattack (rank 3 and up) with its stat down to 2, or any ability with its stat at 0. It keeps its rank but is faded on the sheet and tagged Disabled (hover for why). It can't be rolled or posted, isn't offered in the attack box, and gives no bonuses until the stat is back up.
- Character sheet: the build mode bar now always sits on its own line under the header. Before, on a wide enough sheet it moved up beside the name and the Race, Gender, Alignment and Level boxes.

## 0.4.0

- Character builder: a step-by-step window for building a character, with Race, Stats, Abilities, Skills and Details steps. It opens by itself when a character is created (for whoever made it) and when a character's level goes up (for the character's players who are online, or whoever raised the level if none are). It can also be opened from the character sheet. Steps can be taken in any order from the list at the top, and a bar under it shows the stat points, ability points and skills left and the stat cap. Every change is made to the character straight away, under the same rules as the sheet, so closing the builder part way loses nothing. Finish closes it and takes the character out of build mode.
- Race step: the race's name and the character's racial skills, with the list of racial skills to add underneath. Racial skills that can't be taken (the limit of 3 is reached, it's already taken, or Flight rules it out) are greyed out, and hovering shows why.
- Stats step: + and − buttons for each stat. Each rank costs a stat point, and no stat can go above the stat cap for the character's level.
- Abilities step: the character's abilities, grouped into Full Actions, Supplemental and Passive, with + and − buttons for their rank. The abilities that can be taken are listed underneath with what they cost. Ones that can't be taken yet are greyed out with the reason, such as "Needs MIG 3" or "Not enough ability points". Hovering over an ability shows its effect.
- Skills step: the character's skills, and the skills to add, grouped by their compendium folder. A skill can also be added by typing its name. Skills added by a Profession are marked and go with it.
- Details step: name, gender, alignment, and the profession's name and a brief description.
- On the Race, Abilities and Skills steps, what the character has stays in a box at the top, and only the list to pick from underneath scrolls.
- Abilities, skills and racial skills to pick from come from every Item compendium and from the world's Items directory, so homebrew can be added there. The Characters (SRD) compendium has a new Abilities folder in Character Features for the system's abilities.
- Character progression: what each level gives is a table the GM can change under Game Settings → Configure Progression, one row per level from 1 to 10. The default:
  - All stats and abilities start at rank 0. A level 1 character has 4 stat points, 4 ability points and 3 skills, and stats are capped at rank 2.
  - Each level after that gives 1 stat point and 3 ability points.
  - The stat cap goes up to 3 at level 3, 4 at level 6 and 5 at level 9, and levels 3, 6 and 9 each give an extra skill.
  - Points that aren't spent carry over to later levels. Reset to Defaults fills the table back in without saving until Save is clicked.
- Each stat rank and each ability rank costs one point. What racial skills give comes on top and doesn't use points: the rank from Stat Increase, the rank from Ability, and the skill from Profession. A racial increase is still limited by the stat cap, so Might at 2 on a level 1 character can't take Stat Increase (Might) to reach 3.
- Build mode: a character's player can only change its stats, level, ability ranks, ability stats and types, and which abilities, skills and racial skills it has while the character is in build mode. Names, effect text and stamina costs can be changed at any time, and the GM can always change anything. New characters start in build mode, and raising a character's level puts it in build mode. The hammer button next to Level enters it and opens the builder.
- While a character is in build mode, a bar under its sheet's header shows the points left and the stat cap, with buttons to open the builder and to finish building. A count turns red if more has been spent than earned, such as after the level was lowered.
- In build mode, players can't spend more points than they have, raise a stat above the cap, or lower a stat below what one of their abilities needs. A warning says why, and for a stat it names the abilities that need it.
- Free respec, in the Character Progression settings (on by default): while building or levelling up, points spent at earlier levels can be taken back and spent again. When it's off, finishing building locks in what the character has: its stats and ability ranks can't go below that, and its abilities, skills and racial skills can't be removed, except by the GM.
- Lock character building, a new world setting (off by default): players can't enter build mode or change their character's level, so the GM levels characters up. Raising the level still puts the character in build mode, so its player can spend the new points. While it's on, a player with a character that has been built before gets only the Stats, Abilities and Skills steps in the builder, and finishing with points left over asks first, since they can't come back to spend them until the GM lets them. A character that has never finished building always gets every step.
- Abilities now have an action type: Full Action takes the character's turn and is rolled against its stat and rank; Supplemental is added onto a full action, such as an attack; Passive is always on. Only full actions are rolled. Clicking a supplemental or passive ability posts it to chat with its type. The character sheet's Abilities list has a section for each type, each with its own + button, and the basic Melee, Ranged and Magic attacks are full actions.
- An ability can be tied to more than one stat, picked with checkboxes on its sheet; the Stat column on the character sheet lists them, with the highest underlined. Its rank can't go above the highest of those stats, and a full action rolls with that one. If an effect lowers the stat, the ability keeps its rank but works as if it were the lower rank: the rank box turns red, hovering explains, and the success chances use the lower rank.
- Special: supplemental abilities have a Special checkbox on their sheet. A character can have several Special abilities, but only one can be added onto the same attack; they can still be combined with other supplemental abilities. Special abilities are tagged "Special" on the sheet, in the builder and in chat. The attack box enforces the one-per-attack rule.
- Abilities have a Min Rank and Max Rank (1 and 5 by default). Taking an ability costs its minimum rank in ability points, so an ability with a minimum and maximum of 3 costs 3 points and needs a stat of 3. Its rank must be 0 (not taken) or between the two.
- Stat Increase now raises the stat it's for by one rank as soon as it's taken, and the stat shows "(+1 racial)" in the builder. That rank can't be taken off by hand; removing the racial skill takes it back, unless an ability needs it. Changing the stat on the skill's sheet moves the rank to the new stat. Stat Increases a character already had aren't changed, since their rank was added by hand before.
- Stats already at the stat cap aren't offered when picking a Stat Increase.
- The Ability racial skill now asks which ability it's for when it's taken: one the character has that can go a rank higher, or a new ability with a minimum rank of 1. It gives that ability a rank, and the ability is shown next to the racial skill's name, such as "Ability (Cleave)". Removing the racial skill takes the rank back. Each copy must be for a different ability. On a character, the racial skill's sheet has an Ability box to pick or change which ability it's for, and the rank moves with it. An Ability already on a character still counts as a free ability rank; pick its ability on its sheet to apply the rank.
- Character sheet: the stat boxes show each stat as allocated, without effects. The success chances next to them still include effects. Before, saving the sheet while an effect changed a stat wrote that change into the stat for good.
- SRD abilities: the Abilities folder in Characters (SRD) now has Increase Damage (Melee, Ranged, Magic), Strike Speed (Melee, Ranged), Speed Caster, Feint, Bludgeon, Puncture, Ranged Caster, Close Quarter Ranger, Lacerate, Physical Defense, Magic Defense, Counterattack, Skill Boost, Impose Condition: Restrained and Impose Condition.
- Abilities have a Basic Attacks box on their sheet: checkboxes for the Melee, Ranged and Magic attacks the ability works with. An ability's stats don't have to match the attack's: Feint is a melee ability that uses Agility. The three basic attacks are now shown as "Basic" on the sheet and in chat, and still follow the full-action rules.
- Scaling: abilities have a value that grows with rank. It is set by a starting value at the ability's minimum rank, how much it goes up, and whether it goes up every rank, every other rank (ranks 3 and 5), or not at all. The value for the current rank is shown next to the effect on the character sheet, on the ability's sheet, and in chat, such as "(Currently +3)". Full actions now show their effect under the roll in chat, as monster abilities already did.
- Abilities that apply themselves: Increase Damage adds its rank to the damage of its basic attack, and Physical Defense and Magic Defense add theirs to PDR and MDR. They use the rank as limited by the stat, so an effect lowering the stat lowers the bonus too. The damage, PDR and MDR boxes show the total, and hovering over them shows where it comes from. Hits applied from chat use the total.
- Only the GM can change a character's PDR, MDR and basic attack damage. The GM types the total wanted, and the ability bonus is taken off before saving.
- Impose Condition asks which condition it imposes when a character takes it, and is renamed after it, such as "Impose Condition (Bleeding)". It can be taken once for each condition. Any ability can be set up this way with the "Choose a condition when taken" checkbox on its sheet.
- Abilities that don't scale are fixed at one rank: Close Quarter Ranger at rank 1, and Counterattack and Impose Condition: Restrained at rank 3.
- Attack box: clicking a basic attack (Melee, Ranged or Magic) first opens a box listing the character's supplemental abilities for that attack, with Attack and Cancel buttons. Abilities that cost stamina, Special abilities and limited abilities such as Feint have a checkbox; only one Special can be checked. Free abilities such as Bludgeon, Puncture and Ranged Caster always apply and are listed underneath. The box shows the stamina the checked abilities cost, and Attack is greyed out when the character doesn't have enough. Attack then opens the usual roll box with the conditions' modifiers and Normal / Advantage / Disadvantage.
- Stamina is taken when the attack is rolled, so backing out of the roll box costs nothing.
- Extra attacks: Strike Speed and Speed Caster add attacks, and the attack box opens again for each one. What was picked on the first attack carries over and is only paid for once. Only limited abilities are picked again on each attack: Feint while it has uses left this turn (1, 2 or 3 attacks, by rank), and Impose Condition: Restrained until it lands on a hit, since it only affects one target. Cancelling part way asks before giving up the remaining attacks.
- In combat, the attack box remembers what was checked and which attack is next until the character's turn ends, so if it's closed by accident (the X or Esc), clicking the attack again picks up where it left off. Cancel gives the attack up. Out of combat, closing it drops the attack.
- Supplemental abilities on an attack apply themselves through the draggable hit in chat: Bludgeon and Puncture let the hit ignore that much of the target's DR (shown on the hit and in the result), Lacerate inflicts its Bleeding, and Impose Condition inflicts its condition (its value in stacks for a stacking one; Restrained is just turned on). Stacks of the same condition from two abilities add up. Feint gives the attack advantage in the roll box. The chat card lists the abilities used and the stamina paid, and the effect text of any the system doesn't apply by itself, such as Counterattack or Close Quarter Ranger.
- Abilities have new Applies Automatically options for this: "Hit ignores this much DR", "Hit inflicts its condition" (with a Condition box), "Gives the attack advantage" and "Adds this many attacks". Supplemental abilities also have an Attack Limit: none (carries over to extra attacks), its value in attacks per turn, or until it lands one hit. Copies of the SRD abilities already on characters get these settings when the GM loads the world.
- Existing worlds are converted when the GM loads them. Abilities with a stat become full actions tied to that stat, and abilities with no stat become passive. Existing characters start out of build mode. Monster abilities aren't affected.

## 0.3.4

- Token HUD: fixed conditions with stacks losing their name label and orange highlight when Monk's Little Details is used. The labels now stay while a condition has stacks, and the red stack count sits on the corner of the condition's icon.
- Cornered Rats Initiative System: a combatant who dies during their own turn, such as from Bleeding at its start, now finishes that turn, and the turn passes to the other side as if they had clicked Finish turn. Before, the turn stayed stuck on their side.
- Combat tracker: the Cancel turn button is now a red "no" symbol (a circle with a line through it) instead of an X.
- Restrained: now forces disadvantage only on ability checks (abilities and basic attacks), not on stat checks. A monster's Check counts as a stat check. Poisoned still affects both.
- Applying hits from chat: every attack that hits shows its damage, damage type and the conditions it inflicts in a dashed box that can be dragged onto a token or an actor sheet. Dropping it takes the target's PDR (physical) or MDR (magical) off the damage, applies the rest, and posts what the target took to chat. Damage with no type asks whether it's physical or magical. Basic Melee and Ranged attacks are physical, and basic Magic is magical. Each attack of a multi-attack ability has its own box, so hits can go to different targets.
- Conditions on a hit are read from a monster ability's Effect text, such as "Poisoned for 1d4 turns", "2 stacks of Bleeding" or "the target is Restrained". A stacking condition takes the number or dice written just after it (1 if there's none), rolled again for each target it's dropped on; other conditions are just turned on. The box lists what it found, so check it before dragging.
- Stacks from a hit don't add up: the largest number applies. A target with 4 stacks of Poisoned that is hit for 1 more stays at 4, and one with 2 that is hit for 4 goes up to 4. This applies to every condition with stacks (Bleeding, Blind, Poisoned, Blessed), and the chat message says when the stacks went up or stayed the same. Clicking a condition in the Token HUD still adds or removes one stack at a time.
- Characters have PDR and MDR boxes, like monsters. The Vitals column is now four rows, lining up with the four stats: Speed and Wounds, PDR and MDR, Health, then Stamina.
- Players can drop hits onto tokens they don't own (such as monsters); the GM's client applies the change, so a GM must be connected. The system now uses a socket for this, so restart the world (return to setup and launch it again) after updating.
- Stat Increase: changing the stat on the skill's sheet no longer offers stats the character already picked for another Stat Increase, so the same stat can't be taken twice.
- Profession: dropping it onto a character opens a box to pick a skill from the compendium or type the name of any skill. The skill is added to the character's skill list and shown next to the racial skill's name, such as "Profession (Lockpicking)". Skills the character already has aren't offered. A typed name that matches a compendium skill uses that skill and its description. The two are linked: removing the Profession removes its skill, and renaming the skill renames it in the brackets. Any racial skill can work this way by setting its "Choose on drop" option to "A skill". A Profession that was already on a character has no skill: remove it and add it again from the compendium to pick one.

## 0.3.3

- New No Quarter splash screen, used as the system's picture in the Foundry setup screen and as the background of the world join page.
- Conditions that stack (Bleeding, Blind, Poisoned, Blessed) now show their stack count on the token itself, not just in the Token HUD: a red circle in the corner of the condition's icon, the same as the one in the HUD. It shows from 1 stack up. Conditions that don't stack have no counter.
- Monster sheet: a Size box in the header, between Creature type and Boss, with the D&D/Pathfinder sizes: Tiny (½ square), Small and Medium (1), Large (2), Huge (3) and Gargantuan (4). Choosing a size sets the size of the monster's tokens; on a token's own sheet it resizes that token. Tokens already on a scene from the monster's main sheet keep their size. New monsters are Medium, and so are existing ones until a size is chosen (their tokens aren't changed until then).
- Monster sheet: the Wounds checkbox is gone from the header, and the Wounds count is now a box of its own between Health and PDR, starting at 0. Only a boss with the Wounds boon uses wounds, and they work as they do for characters: when its health reaches 0 it gains a wound, its health resets to full and the extra damage carries over, and each wound makes its rolls worse. Unlike a character, it dies at 3 wounds, and the box shows "/ 3" beside it. Any other monster dies when its health reaches 0, its Wounds box has no effect and its tokens show no wound counter. Monsters that had the old checkbox ticked need the Wounds boon to keep using wounds.
- Monster sheet: the three success chance boxes on the Stats line are a fixed width instead of stretching across the column.
- Monster sheet: a Boss checkbox at the end of the header. A boss gets a Boons section under its abilities. Boons are a new item type: a name and a description. Drag them onto a boss from the compendium, or add one with the + button and name it in the list. The description shows under the name; edit it on the boon's sheet, and click the icon to post the boon to chat. The section title shows how many boons a boss of that level gets: 1 for levels 1–5, 2 for levels 6–10 and 3 from level 11. This number is a guide, and a boss can have more or fewer. Boons can't be dropped onto characters or onto monsters that aren't bosses. Unticking Boss hides the boons but keeps them.
- Compendiums: the Monsters (SRD) pack of monster actors is renamed Bestiary (SRD). Monsters (SRD) is now a new pack for items that go on monsters, with a Boss Boons folder holding the 8 boss boons: Ablative Armor, Collateral Damage, Knockback, Powerhouse, Precision Strikes, Reprisal, Sacrifice and Wounds. Wounds and Powerhouse are applied automatically; the others are described in their text and applied by hand for now. A boon's sheet has "Gives wounds" and "Gives advantage" settings that make any boon work like Wounds or Powerhouse.
- Powerhouse: the boss makes every d100 check with advantage. It works like Blessed without using up stacks: the roll box only offers Advantage and names Powerhouse as the reason, and Poisoned or Restrained cancel it out so the roll is made normally. With Blessed as well, the roll still has advantage only once, and a Blessed stack is still used up. An ability that makes several attacks has advantage on all of them.
- A boon can have a minimum level, set on its sheet, and the compendium puts it in the name too: Ablative Armor (level 6+). Dropping the boon onto a lower level boss still adds it, with a warning that it's designed for monsters of that level or higher. While the boss is below that level, a red warning shows next to the boon on its sheet.

## 0.3.2

- Monster sheet: the Damage/Effect column is split into Attacks, Damage and Effect. Attacks is how many attack rolls one use of the ability makes (default 1); a limited-use ability uses up one use for all of them, not one per roll. Damage is dealt by each attack that hits, plus 1 for a Greater success, 2 for Extreme and 5 for a critical success, like the basic attacks. Leave Damage empty for an ability that only has an effect. All the attacks go in one chat card, numbered, with the effect shown once underneath. The roll mode is asked once, but conditions count each attack as its own roll: each attack uses up a Blessed stack, so with 1 stack only the first attack has advantage (or, against Poisoned or Restrained, only the first is made normally and the rest with disadvantage), and ignoring Burning's penalty costs 1 damage per attack. Monster sheets also open a little wider to fit the new columns.
- Existing monster abilities start with 1 attack and an empty Damage box (the old hidden default of 1 is cleared; any other value is kept); their damage is still written in Effect, so move it across to have it rolled. The first time the GM loads the world, every ability's Attacks and Damage are saved, so a Damage value entered on an older ability no longer disappears on the next load.
- Character sheet: the basic Melee, Ranged and Magic attacks have their own icons. Existing characters' basic attacks switch over the next time their sheet is opened, unless someone already gave them a different picture.
- Character sheet: a Breather button under the portrait. It restores Health and Stamina to full and removes every condition. Wounds stay as they are, and so does Dead, since it follows the wounds. It's greyed out and can't be used while the character is in a combat that has started.
- Cornered Rats Initiative System: once a combat has started, players can only move a token while it's the combatant taking their turn (after Start turn, until Finish or Cancel). Moving any other token, including one left out of the combat, is blocked with a warning. The GM can still move any token. (#5)
- Restrained: now also forces disadvantage on every stat and ability check, like Poisoned. Against Blessed the two cancel out and the roll is made normally. (#4)
- Skills are now a plain list of names on the Skills tab, with no stat, success chances or Trained checkbox, and they aren't rolled. When a skill helps, the GM gives advantage and the player makes a normal stat roll. Skills come from the new Skills folder in the SRD compendium (drag them onto the sheet), or can be added with the + button and named directly in the list. A character can't have the same skill twice. Each skill has a sheet with a description.
- Existing characters are converted when the GM loads the world: basic skills they were trained in, and skills they had added themselves, become skills in the new list (linked to the compendium entry where there is one). Untrained basic skills are dropped, since the list now only holds the skills a character actually has.
- Racial skills: a new item type, dragged onto a character from the compendium. Dropping one anywhere on the sheet adds it to a new Racial Skills section at the top of the Skills tab, above Profession. A character can have up to 3; monsters can't have them. A skill can only be taken twice if it's marked as repeatable (such as Faster Movement or Health), and one marked as excluding all others (Flight) can't be taken alongside any other racial skill. Each row shows a summary of what the skill gives.
- Stat Increase: dropping it onto a character opens a box to pick which stat it's for, and the stat is shown next to its name, such as "Stat Increase (Might)". Stats the character already picked for another Stat Increase aren't offered. The stat can be changed later on the skill's sheet. It only records the choice; the stat itself is still raised by hand. Any racial skill can be set up this way with its "Choose on drop" option.
- A racial skill can add to maximum Health, maximum Stamina and Speed, and grant Darkvision, Earthsense or Echolocation (60 ft.). These apply automatically while the character has the skill and go away when it's removed. Stat increases and other character creation bonuses go in its description and are still allocated by hand.
- Character sheet: the Speed box now shows Speed including racial skill bonuses (hover for the breakdown). Editing it changes the base Speed. Health and Stamina maximums show a tooltip when a racial skill adds to them.
- Vision from racial skills is applied to the character's tokens without changing their saved settings, so removing the skill returns the tokens to their own vision. Darkvision gives the darkvision look and sight out to 60 ft. (a token that already sees further keeps its range). Earthsense uses Foundry's tremorsense, which can't detect flying or hovering creatures. Echolocation is a new detection mode: it works in the dark, is blocked by walls, and can detect flying creatures.
- New No Quarter (SRD) compendium folder, with a Characters pack and a Monsters pack. The Characters pack has an Items folder and a Character Features folder, with Racial Skills inside it.
- The Skills folder has the 23 skills with their descriptions, sorted into Might, Agility, Mind and Personality Skills. Intimidation has a Might and a Personality version.
- The Racial Skills folder has the 14 racial skills, sorted into Sensory, Movement, Stats and Abilities, and Miscellaneous. Darkvision, Earthsense, Echolocate, Faster Movement, Health and Stamina apply their effects automatically; the rest (such as Swimming, Pounce or Stat Increase) are described in their text and applied by hand for now. The Monsters pack is still empty.

## 0.3.0

- Character sheet: the Description tab is replaced by a Skills tab. Monsters keep their Description tab.
- Skills tab: a Profession section across the top, with a short box for the profession's name and a longer one for a brief description.
- Every character has the basic skills, grouped by stat and sorted by name: Might and Mind in the left column, Agility and Personality in the right. Intimidation appears twice on purpose, once under Might and once under Personality. Each skill has a Trained checkbox and shows its stat's Reg/Great/Ext success chances.
- Each stat's heading has a + button to add a skill of the character's own under that stat, with an editable name and a delete button.
- Clicking a skill's success chances rolls its stat under the skill's name. A trained skill is rolled with advantage; a source of disadvantage (such as Poisoned, or a situational one by choosing Normal) cancels it out and the roll is made normally. Blessed isn't used up on a trained skill's roll, since it already has advantage.

## 0.2.4

- Actor and item sheets rebuilt on Foundry's ApplicationV2 framework, replacing the old sheet framework that Foundry is phasing out. They look and work as before, except that they always use the light theme. Also clears the "deprecated global" warnings from the console.
- Effects tab: effect icons show again. They had been blank since Foundry renamed the icon field.
- Posting a passive ability (or an item with no roll formula) to chat now follows the chosen roll mode, such as GM or private roll. Before, these cards were always public.

## 0.2.3

- Cornered Rats Initiative System: the GM can no longer start a combatant's turn when it isn't their side's turn, so monsters can't go during the players' turn (and vice versa). To fix a mistake, the GM hands the turn to the other side first. (#1)
- Restrained: an on/off condition that sets the creature's Speed to 0 while active. The Speed box on the sheet shows 0 and is locked; the normal Speed comes back when the condition is removed. Any movement on a Restrained combatant's turn shows red on the movement ruler.
- Monsters now have a Speed (default 30), shown in the stat block between MDR and Stats. The movement ruler colors monster movement against it too.
- Blessed: a stacking condition that grants advantage on the next roll, with no opting out. It doesn't count down at the end of a turn; instead it loses one stack each time it's applied to a roll. Against a condition that forces disadvantage (such as Poisoned) the two cancel out and the roll is made normally, still using up one Blessed stack.

## 0.2.2

- Character sheet: Health, Stamina and Wounds moved out of the header to a new Vitals list next to Stats, alongside a new Speed field. The header now has Race, Gender and Alignment text fields where those used to sit.
- Movement ruler: while it's a combatant's active turn, their token's movement line and grid highlight are colored green up to their Speed, yellow up to double Speed, and red beyond that. Starting a turn resets the token's movement history so the coloring reflects only that turn's movement.
- Canceling a turn now puts the token back where it stood when the turn started, undoing any movement made during it.
- The Reg/Great/Ext success chances (Stats and Abilities) are now read-only boxes styled like the Health/Stamina max field, with the header labels lined up against the boxes below them.

## 0.2.1

- Condition markers are now stackable. In the Token HUD, left-click a status effect to add a stack (creating it if it isn't already active); right-click removes one, clearing the condition once it reaches 0. The current count shows as a badge on the icon.
- Bleeding: deals damage equal to its stack count at the start of the bleeding character's turn, and loses one stack at the end of it. Canceling a turn (or having it interrupted, e.g. by combat moving on) undoes any health and wounds change it caused.
- Blind: a duration in turns rather than a severity, losing one stack at the end of each turn. Gives a -10 success penalty to stat and ability checks; the roll dialog shows a checkbox (checked by default) so a check that doesn't rely on sight can opt out.
- Poisoned: a duration in turns like Blind. Forces every stat and ability check to be made with disadvantage while it's active, with no opting out.
- Burning: an on/off condition that lasts until healed or cured (not yet implemented). Gives a flat -25 success penalty to checks; the roll dialog lets you decline that penalty instead, at the cost of 1 damage.

## 0.2.0

- Turn order: there is no initiative roll any more. When the combat begins the GM declares which side attacks first, and the sides then alternate turns; there are no rounds. The side that is up chooses who goes, and a combatant cannot go again until everyone else on their side has gone, at which point that side unlocks. Character actors are always on the players' side, and monsters are on the monsters' side unless their token is friendly (an ally).
- Combat tracker: combatants are grouped by side. Players start, end or cancel their own turn; the GM can do this for anyone, mark a combatant as done, let one go again, and give the turn to either side.

## 0.1.1

- Clicking a stat or ability now asks whether the roll is Normal, with Advantage, or with Disadvantage. Advantage rolls twice and keeps the lower result; disadvantage rolls twice and keeps the higher. Wounds apply to both rolls.
- Monster abilities: uses count down per combat and reset when the combat ends; the range gets " ft." added when a bare number is typed; the chat card shows the damage type and range after the name and the damage/effect under the roll.
- Monster sheet: the Health, PDR, MDR and Stats line is its own block above the abilities; the 100 cap on the Stats chances is gone; XP was removed.

## 0.1

- First public version: characters with stats and abilities, and monsters with a single-stat stat block (level, alignment, creature type, health, PDR/MDR, and abilities with a damage type, range, and uses per combat).
